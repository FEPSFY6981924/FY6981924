Introduction

As of 2026, Humanity has not found any trace of life outside our home planet (NASA, 2026). Whilst it appears that other celestial bodies could have the potential to host life (Norris, 2026), these entities are relatively rare in the vast expanse of the universe (Bohl et al., 2026) but although outer space is often barren and hostile, microgravity presents a unique opportunity to conduct experiments that would be impossible on the Earth (NASA, 2022) Many of these experiments have huge benefits to the general public (NASA, 2022), in many different industries (NASA, 2022. Saldana, 2025). Examples of this include medicine and pharmaceuticals (Mulligan, Et al., 2025), material synthesis (ISS National Lab, 2024), and agriculture (NASA, 2025). However, these experiments come with incredible risks, and long-term effect to astronauts’ health. (Gray, 2024). This essay will aim to discuss some of the risks and benefits associated with carrying out research in microgravity, and to impart an understanding of why, despite the dangers, it is crucial that we continue to conduct research in space, for the benefit of humanity as a whole.
